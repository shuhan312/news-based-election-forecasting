# Feature selection: what the table can and cannot support

> **Historical record.** These findings profile the removed ward-level
> pilot feature chain, whose code and outputs are retired to Git history;
> the by-election splitting item tracked here was subsequently addressed
> by the Stage 1 chronological split design
> (`surrey-election-no-news-baseline/docs/candidate_split_and_leakage.md`).
> Not part of the final feature pipeline.

Recorded 29 July 2026. Every figure below was produced by
`news_modelling.run_feature_selection` against
`news_features/ward_party_election_features_v1/ward_party_election_features.parquet`
and can be regenerated by rerunning it. Outputs are in
`news_features/feature_selection_v1/`.

This document exists because building the selection layer turned up several
things about the feature table that are more consequential than the selection
rules themselves. The findings are recorded first and the method second.

---

## 1. The headline finding: the validation split has no news

| Split | Rows | Rows with any non-zero news signal |
|---|---:|---:|
| `historical_training` | 3,418 | **324** (9.5%) |
| `validation_2021` | 1,639 | **0** (0.0%) |
| `final_test_2026` | 1,262 | 1,262 (100%) |
| `secondary_test_2026_07` | 4 | 0 (0.0%) |

By election year, non-zero news exists in exactly two places:

- **2017**: 324 of 1,627 rows
- **2026**: 1,262 of 1,266 rows
- 2013: none. 2021: none. Every by-election: none.

### Why this matters more than anything else in this document

The central comparison of the project is baseline versus baseline-plus-news,
and the design puts that comparison on the 2021 validation set. On a split
where every news feature is zero or missing, the baseline model and the news
model receive **identical inputs** and must therefore produce **identical
predictions**. Their scores will match exactly.

That match is not evidence that news does not help. It is a restatement of the
fact that there is no news in the 2021 rows. Reporting it as a finding would be
a straightforward error, and it is an error that would be easy to make because
the numbers would look perfectly reasonable — two models, two scores, no
difference.

`split_coverage()` was added to the selection module for this reason. It is a
precondition check, not a summary: it is meant to be run before any model
comparison is believed, and it flags `news_comparison_possible = False` for
2021 and for the July 2026 secondary test.

### What follows from it

As things stand the news arm has **training data from one election (2017) and
no validation data at all**. Three ways out, none of them free:

1. **Extract the pending corpus.** 3,584 eligible articles have never been
   through extraction; the alignment layer still names its dataset
   `context-cards-v1.0-pilot67`. Section 5 confirms 836 of those are 2021, so
   this route does reach the validation split.
2. **Move the validation split** to an election that has news. This changes
   the chronological design and is not a decision to take unilaterally.
3. **Report the news comparison on 2026 only**, and state plainly that the
   validation split could not carry it.

Option 1 is the only one that does not weaken the design, and section 5
establishes that the articles it needs exist. What it does not yet establish is
how many of them fall inside a principal window, which is the remaining
measurable unknown and is measurable for free.

## 2. The table is far emptier on training rows than its width suggests

Of 12,065 candidate columns, screened against the 3,418 training rows on shape
alone:

| Stage | Columns removed |
|---|---:|
| Empty on every training row | 9,641 |
| One distinct value | 2,230 |
| Exact duplicate of another column | 145 |
| **Surviving** | **49** |

The same screen run on all 6,323 rows leaves 620 columns. The gap between 620
and 49 is entirely down to *where* the news sits: most populated columns are
populated only in 2026, and a column with no training values cannot be learned
from however well populated it is elsewhere.

Both screens read only the shape of a column — is it empty, is it constant, is
it a copy — and never an outcome, so neither can leak.

## 3. Local news contributes nothing on training rows

Selecting within the `local` specification: **5,399 candidate columns, zero
local columns selected.** Every local news column is empty or constant across
the training rows; the five columns the local specification ends up with are
its baseline and coverage columns, which it shares with every other
specification.

Consequently the `full` specification selects exactly what `national` selects —
17 identical columns — because local adds nothing for it to choose from.

**This is a fact about the pilot, not about local news.** Section 5 traces why:
almost nothing in the extracted pilot falls inside a principal window at all,
and what little does is too thin to survive screening in the local blocks. It
would be wrong to write down "local Surrey news has no predictive value": the
correct statement is that the current corpus contains no extracted local
coverage on any training-period contest, so the question has not been tested.

The block-budget rule in the selection module exists precisely so that this
stays a statement about data rather than becoming an artefact of the selection
rule — if local and national competed for the same slots, local would lose on
volume alone and its absence would look like a finding.

## 4. Selection results per specification

Against `target__party_vote_share`, 3,418 training rows:

| Specification | Considered | Selected | Training rows per feature |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| baseline | 83 | 5 | 684 |
| local | 5,399 | 5 | 684 |
| national | 5,399 | 17 | 201 |
| combined | 1,433 | 10 | 342 |
| full | 10,715 | 17 | 201 |

The budget ceiling — one feature per ten training rows, so 341 — never binds.
Structural screening and redundancy pruning cut far below it on their own. The
overfitting risk the layer was built to address turns out not to be the live
one; the live one is that there is very little to fit on.

### The strongest associations, and why they should not be trusted yet

| Rank correlation | Training rows | Column |
|---:|---:|---|
| 0.737 | 243 | `combined__30_to_8_complete_days__competence_positive_n` |
| 0.703 | 324 | `combined__information_available_at_30_days__consq_credible_challenger_signal_n` |
| 0.502 | 459 | `baseline__predicted_party_vote_share` |
| 0.494 | 324 | `combined__information_available_at_30_days__consequence_denom` |

A rank correlation of 0.74 with vote share is a large number and it is the kind
of number that ends up in a summary. It should not, yet:

- Every non-missing value sits in **one election, 2017**. This is a
  within-election association, not a pattern established across elections, and
  the design is explicitly about predicting elections the model has not seen.
- 243 rows is a modest base, and the news features are counts with long right
  tails, where a handful of heavily covered contests carry the estimate.
- The two news columns above outrank the frozen Stage 1 prediction, which is
  implausible on its face and is the clearest signal that these are artefacts
  of a 67-article pilot rather than measurements of anything.

None of these columns has been near a fitted model. They are candidates.

## 5. Why the table is empty: nine articles, not sixty-seven

Tracing the emptiness back through the pipeline gives a sharper answer than
"the pilot is small", and one that changes what to do about it.

The extracted pilot is 67 articles and it is **evenly spread across all four
elections** — 16 for 2013, 16 for 2017, 16 for 2021, 19 for 2026. It is not
missing 2021, and it is not skewed towards any one election.

What it is missing is articles close to polling day. Dating each pilot article
against its election's polling date:

| Election | Pilot articles | Inside the 1–30 day principal windows |
|---|---:|---:|
| SCC-2013-05 | 16 | 2 |
| SCC-2017-05 | 16 | 3 |
| SCC-2021-05 | 16 | **1** |
| ESWS-2026-05 | 19 | 3 |
| **Total** | **67** | **9** |

The other 58 sit 49 to 150-plus days before polling — outside every principal
window, and outside the 30-day cumulative snapshot as well. They were collected
on a 180-day horizon and the windows are 30 days deep, so most of the pilot was
never going to reach a feature.

**Nine articles is the real size of the extracted evidence base**, and one of
them is 2021's. That is why section 1 finds no news on the validation split: it
is not that 2021 was skipped, it is that 2021's single in-window article does
not survive into a non-zero feature on any row.

### What this changes

The earlier reading — pilot skews national, so local looks empty — was wrong,
and the corrected reading is more encouraging. The pending corpus covers every
election:

| Election | Eligible, never extracted |
|---|---:|
| SCC-2013-05 | 1,136 |
| SCC-2017-05 | 895 |
| **SCC-2021-05** | **836** |
| ESWS-2026-05 | 717 |
| **Total** | **3,584** |

Split by arm: 2,190 national and 1,394 local. For 2021 alone: 446 national and
390 local.

So the open question at the end of section 1 — does extraction actually reach
the validation split — has a verified answer: **yes**. 836 eligible 2021
articles are waiting, roughly balanced across both arms. Extraction is
therefore the step that would give the validation split news to compare on,
rather than a step that would leave the design problem in place.

What is not yet verified is how many of those 3,584 fall inside the principal
windows. The pilot's ratio was 9 in 67, and if that ratio held it would leave
roughly 480 in-window articles across four elections. The pilot was not drawn
to be representative, so the ratio may not hold either way — but it is
measurable from the stored dates without spending anything, and it should be
measured before the extraction budget is committed rather than after.

## 7. Two data problems found along the way

**Fifty-two training rows post-date the validation set.** `historical_training`
contains by-elections from 2022, 2023 and 2025 — including three on 2025-10-16
— while the validation split is the 2021 county election. The stated design is
train 2013–2019, validate 2021, test 2026, and by-elections after 2021 do not
fit it. Training on 2025 and validating on 2021 is training on the future to
predict the past.

It is 52 of 3,418 rows and **all 52 carry no news at all**, so it does not
touch anything in this document. It does affect the Stage 1 baseline, which was
fitted on those rows. Recorded here because it was found here; it belongs to
the split definition rather than to selection.

**`election_date` is empty for every by-election row** in the feature table,
while `election_id` carries the date in its text. The first check of the
chronology above used `election_date`, found no post-2021 rows, and was wrong;
redoing it against the dates parsed out of `election_id` found the 52. Any
downstream code that filters or sorts by `election_date` will silently drop or
misplace by-elections.

## 8. How selection works

Two tiers, split by a single criterion: whether the rule reads the outcome.

**Tier one is structural** — empty, constant, near-constant, too sparse, exact
duplicate. These questions are answered without reading a vote share, so their
answers cannot carry information about the outcome.

**Tier two ranks by association with the outcome**, and therefore reads
training rows only. Computing it on rows the model is later scored on would put
those rows' results into the feature set, and the holdout score would then be
optimistic by an amount that cannot be recovered afterwards.
`rank_by_association()` raises if handed any row outside the training split, so
this is enforced rather than documented. The test suite asserts it from the
other direction as well: replacing every validation outcome with noise leaves
the selected columns unchanged.

Both tiers count on training rows. A column with values only in the 2026
holdout is not a usable predictor however complete it looks overall.

**Ranking uses Spearman rather than Pearson.** News features are counts with
long right tails, and Pearson on that shape is driven by the few largest
values. Rank correlation asks whether more-covered contests tend to sit higher
on the outcome, which is the question worth asking.

**Each block gets its own budget**, allocated by water-filling: a block with
fewer candidates than its fair share keeps all of them and returns the
remainder to the pool. Small blocks survive whole, large blocks split the
surplus. Ranked together, national's 584 surviving columns would take every
slot and local would be absent by construction.

**Selection runs per specification, not once globally**, because the blocks
overlap: `combined` already contains the national coverage. One global list
would show a model the same articles twice under two names and split their
coefficient across two columns that are not independent.

## 9. Status

The layer is complete and tested: 38 tests, and the 118 existing news-side
tests still pass. What it produces is a defensible column list for each of the
five specifications and a written reason for every column left out.

What it cannot do is make the table say more than it contains. The binding
constraint on the news arm is not selection or model choice — it is that news
exists on 324 training rows from a single election and on no validation row at
all.
